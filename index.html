✒ Article

Responsible Social Media Use: A Call for Digital Responsibility

An awareness article for a general readership on fact-checking, respectful engagement, privacy, and screen-time balance — written to be clear, direct, and easy to act on.

Social media has become a significant part of life today, where people share information, communicate with each other and learn about what is happening in the world around them. But as it becomes more influential, users are encouraged to use social media responsibly and make the web a safer and more positive place. A major aspect of being responsible when using social media is fact checking the information before sharing it. Online, false information and misleading content can quickly proliferate, leading to uncertainty and senseless panic. Users are encouraged to verify information by going to reliable sources before believing or reposting. It's just as essential to be respectful when interacting online. Bad comments, hurtful language and cyberbullying can have a negative impact on someone's health. Internet users should consider before posting, not share harmful information, and be nice and respectful to others. It's also important to take measures to protect personal data. When users reveal important information, it can make them vulnerable to scams and other online threats, including their home addresses, passwords, and private information. The user should check his/her privacy settings and remain careful when meeting people on the Internet. Moreover, it is important to have a healthy balance between online activities and real-life responsibilities. Excessive social media use can impact productivity, sleep and relationships. Time limits and prioritizing work, school, family, and rest can help to foster healthier digital habits. The use of social media is an ongoing phenomenon in society and communication; responsibility for its use lies with everyone. Users can play a role in fostering an informed, respectful, and safe online environment by verifying information, showing respect for others, safeguarding privacy, and managing screen time. Think before you click, check before you share, and be respectful in all communications with others.

✒ Editorial

Essay: "The Influence of Social Media on Youth"

A short essay on how social media shapes young people's mental health, academics, and social behavior — tightened here for structure and repetition while keeping the original argument intact.

Social media has become one of the primary ways young people connect today. Through platforms like Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, and Instagram, they create, share, and exchange information with others across the world. But like any tool that nearly everyone can access, social media carries both benefits and risks — and its influence on how young people think and behave can go either way. Used well, social media can support a young person's mental health, broaden their social perspective, and sharpen how they think. It gives them a way to reconnect with friends and acquaintances beyond face-to-face contact, and it can support their academic work and skill-building. Used poorly, though, the same platforms can pull youth into unethical behavior — sharing content carelessly, posting without thought, or chasing trends just to keep up. The World Health Organization estimates that one in seven adolescents aged 10–19 (14.3%) experiences a mental disorder, and it lists bullying among the recognized risks to their mental health. The pandemic made this more pressing. With young people spending more time in front of screens than ever, two problems stand out. First, prolonged exposure to devices carries its own physical toll. Second, social media content is largely unfiltered — not everything young users encounter is accurate, and many accept what they see without questioning it. That isn't a failure of judgment so much as a failure of guidance: young people need parents and guardians actively involved, monitoring screen time, the platforms they use, and who they interact with online. Social media's influence on youth is undeniable — it supports their studies, their downtime, and their social lives, but it can just as easily work against them. What's needed is balance: an awareness that whatever they share is public, and a responsibility to use these platforms wisely. Above all, young people shouldn't let a screen replace the fullness of the life in front of them. Source: World Health Organization, "Mental health of adolescents" fact sheet.

✒ Creative

East of Eden — A Reflection

A close reading of Steinbeck's East of Eden, written to work through the novel's central ideas — the concept of timshel, the Cain and Abel structure, and the choice each generation of the Trask family is given to do right.

East of Eden is one of those books you read again and again. The central theme is timshel. Lee, the Trask family's servant, spends years studying a single Hebrew word from the Cain and Abel story. Translations render it as "thou shalt," a promise, or "do thou," a command. Lee concludes it means "thou mayest": a choice. That is the book's thesis. Sin is not inevitable, and neither is goodness. Individuals do not have to repeat their fathers' mistakes, and each generation is given the chance to decide again to do right. It is Steinbeck's answer to an old fear: that we are trapped as we were made. The Cain and Abel structure is not subtle, and it isn't meant to be. It repeats across generations, Adam and Charles, then Aron and Caleb, with one son favored and the other rejected, and a father's love, or the lack of it, working like a storm. The characters are acting out a story they don't know they are in, and they face a real decision about how it ends. Steinbeck wants you to watch them. Cathy/Kate is the novel's hardest character, and that is the point. Steinbeck tells you early that she may be a rare person born without a conscience, then makes you think about it for the rest of the book. She isn't a symbol; she's a test. Can a story about the possibility of choosing good hold up if one character has no real option to choose it? The whole theory of the book runs through Caleb. He believes his darkness is a gift from his mother, and it falls to him to decide, with real consequences, whether that darkness is a sentence or a beginning. His arc is where timshel stops being a philosophy and becomes a choice with a cost. Lee is the silent conscience of the cast. For most of the novel he is the Trask family's servant, yet he is also the most self-possessed and clear-minded person in it, the one doing the real work of finding its meaning while everyone else goes through the motions of agony.
